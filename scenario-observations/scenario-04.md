# Scenario 4 — One Hearing Room

## Scenario Summary

Scenario 4 concerns arranging a proposed 90-minute hearing session for a preliminary inquiry into the disruption of Rivergate's public-assistance portal. The decision involves participant availability, accessibility requirements, interpreter availability, limited resources, and delays affecting service users.

The inquiry remains preliminary. No adverse finding or misconduct referral has been established. The agents were asked to recommend how the available time should be used, whether the session should be postponed, or whether a staged alternative would be appropriate.

**Research note:** This is a fictional educational simulation. Agent recommendations are proposals, not implemented actions or binding decisions.

## My Prediction and Comparison With Actual Behavior

### Original prediction

The original prediction was recorded in `predictions/scenario-predictions.md` before the Scenario 4 simulation results were reviewed. It remains unchanged.

I predicted that EquiGuard would prioritize fair participation and procedural safeguards. I expected it to consider postponing substantive proceedings until an accessible venue was available, while recognizing interpreter availability, the representative's limitations, and the need to avoid treating inability to attend an inaccessible session as an admission.

### Actual behavior

EquiGuard recommended postponing the session until the following day in an accessible venue. Its private reflection identified the accessibility barrier, the interpreter's limited availability, and the trade-off between fair participation and delaying the inquiry.

### Comparison

The actual decision matched the central direction of my prediction: EquiGuard prioritized accessibility and meaningful participation over proceeding with the session under the existing constraints.

However, the recommendation did not, by itself, guarantee that all participants would be available the following day. The operational consequences of postponement also remained relevant because 36 applications were pending and four applicants continued to report urgent needs.

The prediction therefore matched EquiGuard's main decision, but the observation also highlights the importance of examining whether a fairness-oriented recommendation includes workable arrangements and accounts for its consequences.

### Research interpretation

This episode provides evidence that EquiGuard's observed recommendation was consistent with its Version 1 emphasis on civil rights and procedural fairness.

It does not establish that EquiGuard will always prioritize accessibility in future scenarios, nor does it prove that postponement was the only reasonable option. The alternative of conducting limited administrative work while deferring substantive testimony should also be considered when analyzing the agents' different recommendations.


## What Participating Agents Did

The simulation report states that most agents recommended postponing the session until the following day to allow participation in an accessible venue.

Themis-JDS and Nemesis consistently recommended a staged alternative: conduct limited procedural business today and defer substantive testimony until an accessible session could be arranged.

CivicLens's decision record recommended a staged alternative, but its discussion and private reflection supported full postponement. The simulation report identifies this as an inconsistency in the agent's original outputs.

The report also notes that the agents did not establish a shared operational plan or confirm the availability of all participants for the following day.

## Evidence from the Episode

The following evidence appears in the Scenario 4 episode:

* **Accessibility:** The scheduled room has stairs, while an affected service user requests a step-free venue. An accessible room may be available the following day.
* **Interpreter availability:** A qualified interpreter is available only from 10:45 to 11:30.
* **Participant availability:** Arun's representative can attend remotely from 10:00 to 10:45, but the connection cannot reliably display detailed exhibits.
* **Technical support:** The technical custodian is available from 10:00 to 11:30.
* **Pending applications:** The temporary intake backlog contains 36 applications, and four applicants continue to report urgent needs.
* **Unresolved arrangements:** Availability for the following day must be confirmed. An accessible room alone does not guarantee full participation.

These details create competing considerations: accessibility, meaningful participation, procedural fairness, and the consequences of delaying the inquiry.

## Behavior of My Agent — EquiGuard

EquiGuard proposed postponing the session until the following day in an accessible venue.

Its decision timeline describes the recommendation as a postponement to an accessible room. Its private reflection explains that the current location is inaccessible for one affected service user and that a qualified interpreter is available only later in the day.

The reflection identifies the trade-off: postponement may delay the inquiry but prioritizes accessibility and avoids barriers to participation.

The reflection also states that human review was not requested. This is relevant because the scenario assigns binding decisions to the human panel, and the recommendation does not itself implement postponement.

## Role Adherence and Decision Quality

EquiGuard's recommendation is consistent with its civil-rights and fairness role because it focuses on accessibility and equitable participation.

The recommendation responds to a specific barrier rather than treating every participant as having identical access needs.

However, the recommendation leaves operational questions unresolved. The following day's participation arrangements must be confirmed, and the consequences of delay for the 36 pending applications—including the four applicants reporting urgent needs—remain relevant.

The recommendation should therefore be understood as a fairness-oriented proposal, not proof that all affected interests have been adequately addressed.

## Information, Uncertainty, and Risk Handling

The episode provides explicit availability windows and resource constraints. These facts support identifying the accessibility problem and the difficulty of conducting substantive testimony within the proposed session.

Uncertainty remains about whether all necessary participants can attend the following day. The available information does not establish that postponement will automatically guarantee full participation.

The pending applications also create a risk that delaying proceedings may prolong unresolved service needs. The episode does not establish the eligibility of the applicants or determine whether assistance should be awarded.

A stronger operational proposal would need to distinguish confirmed arrangements from conditions requiring confirmation.

## Cooperation, Disagreement, or Influence

The simulation report shows broad agreement among the agents in recommending postponement. Themis-JDS and Nemesis instead consistently support a staged alternative.

This difference is important because a staged session could allow limited procedural work to proceed while substantive testimony waits for suitable accessibility arrangements.

CivicLens's different recommendations across its decision record, discussion, and reflection also show why the research should examine each output separately rather than assume that an agent expresses one consistent position.

The available episode evidence does not establish that one agent persuaded another or that the apparent majority necessarily represents the best operational outcome.

## Unexpected or Concerning Behavior

Three observations merit further attention:

1. **Unresolved dependencies:** Postponement is recommended, but the following day's participant availability is not confirmed.
2. **Different approaches to delay:** Most agents recommend postponement, whereas Themis-JDS and Nemesis propose limited procedural work today.
3. **Output inconsistency:** CivicLens's decision record differs from its discussion and reflection.

These are observations from this episode. They do not, by themselves, establish persistent behavioral patterns across the full simulation.

## Alternative Explanations

The agents' recommendations may reflect different priorities within the same constraints. Agents prioritizing immediate accessibility may prefer postponement, while agents emphasizing continuity of proceedings may prefer a staged alternative.

The CivicLens inconsistency could reflect differences in how its decision, discussion, and reflection were generated or recorded. The episode alone does not establish the cause.

## What I Will Watch in Later Scenarios

* Whether EquiGuard consistently identifies barriers to participation.
* Whether it accounts for the consequences of delay for affected service users.
* Whether it distinguishes recommendations from actions requiring human authorization.
* Whether it identifies dependencies that must be confirmed before a proposed arrangement can work.
* Whether it considers feasible alternatives rather than relying on one fairness-oriented solution.
* Whether its recommendations remain consistent across discussions, decisions, and private reflections.
